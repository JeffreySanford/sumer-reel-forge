## Visual Direction

Use cinematic mythic realism grounded in southern Mesopotamian materials and landscapes. Images should feel tactile and lived-in rather than like polished high fantasy.

- Format reel artwork at `9:16`, with important faces and action inside the central safe area.
- Favor water, reeds, bitumen-dark timber, woven reed structures, clay, wool, copper, lapis, and restrained gold accents.
- Use natural dawn and late-afternoon light, visible atmosphere, realistic skin, and detailed practical clothing.
- Avoid Egyptian crowns, Greco-Roman architecture, medieval armor, modern objects, ornamental fantasy weapons, and text generated inside images.
- Keep divine imagery numinous but physical. Supernatural elements should emerge from water, light, wind, or scale rather than spectacle-heavy effects.

## Continuity Anchors

### Enki

- Mature Mesopotamian man with warm brown skin, long black wavy hair, and a full dark beard with subtle gray.
- Cream woven robe with narrow lapis-blue and copper trim, broad copper belt, lapis and copper jewelry, leather sandals.
- Calm, observant authority. He is a traveler and builder in Reel 1, not a warrior.
- The compact horned divine crown from the baseline remains the identity anchor. Keep it restrained in action shots and do not enlarge it into an Egyptian or theatrical silhouette.

### Stag Of The Absu

- Working reed-and-dark-timber sailing vessel with high curved prow and stern.
- Woven shelter, rigging, clay jars, timber bundles, reed bundles, tools, and modest livestock cargo.
- It must read as a practical expedition vessel, not a Viking longship, galleon, or royal barge.

### Nammu

- Primordial feminine presence formed from deep blue water, foam, suspended light, and current.
- Serene and ancient, never horror-coded or rendered as a conventional mermaid.
- She can remain implied rather than fully visible when the narration treats the sea as memory or origin.

### Dilmun

- Luminous low coast with pale stone, clear water, reeds, birds, palms in the distance, and an atmosphere of abundance.
- Distinct from the darker open water at the beginning of the voyage.
- Avoid monumental fantasy cities in the Reel 1 approach shots.

### People And Daily Life

- Families, travelers, and laborers wear practical woven wool or linen garments in mineral and earth colors.
- Show varied ages and individual faces. Treat Martu and other communities as people with agency, skills, families, and material culture.
- Do not use visual shorthand based on dirt, menace, or caricature to signal cultural difference.

The opening should progress from cool black-blue water toward warm Dilmun light. Compose for motion crops and captions: keep the lower 20 percent and outer 8 percent free of critical faces or story details.

## Naming And Source Consistency

- Use `Uruk (Erech)` on first audience-facing mention, then `Uruk`.
- Keep `Eridu` distinct from Uruk; they are separate cities in the story world.
- Use `E-Absu` as the canonical temple name. `E-engura` may appear as an epithet only when the source calls for it.
- Keep Ninsikila, Nintu, and Ninhursag as separate named narrative figures or roles unless a source-specific editorial note explicitly identifies them.
- Record any deliberate modernization, composite identity, or disputed translation in the chapter production notes before rendering.
