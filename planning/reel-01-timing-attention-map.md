# Reel 1 Timing And Attention Map

## Purpose

Map the 60-second Reel 1 structure to viewer attention, narration emphasis, motion intensity, sound, and visual focus so the reel behaves like one directed piece rather than eight independent animated shots.

This document does not replace the authoritative narration or final word timings. Exact cue frames should be derived from the approved narration asset and timing data. The ranges below are editorial targets tied to the current eight-shot structure.

## Governing Principle

Only one thing should dominate viewer attention at a time.

The visual system can contain many moving layers, but each moment should have a clear hierarchy:

1. story idea;
2. primary visual subject;
3. supporting motion;
4. captions;
5. atmosphere and texture.

If atmosphere, parallax, captions, or character motion competes with the current story idea, reduce it.

## Reel Arc

| Time | Shot | Emotional beat | Primary eye target | Motion intensity 0-5 | Visual strategy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 0-6s | Black water | mystery | faint horizon | 1 | minimal information, nearly still |
| 6-13s | Stag voyage | journey | vessel | 3 | lateral travel, physical scale |
| 13-20s | Enki helm | character | Enki face | 2 | intimate push, restrained life |
| 20-28s | Nammu | awe | implied presence | 1 | camera quiets, supernatural coherence emerges |
| 28-36s | Shrine | purpose | water/bread/welcome action | 3 | human practical activity |
| 36-44s | Ideals | meaning | one object/idea at a time | 2 | controlled symbolic progression |
| 44-53s | Dilmun | discovery | coastline | 4 | frame opens, warmth and depth expand |
| 53-60s | Arrival | resolution | boat then title zone | 2 -> 0 | approach, settle, hold |

The reel should not continually increase in motion. It should alternate movement and stillness while gradually increasing warmth, openness, and purpose.

## Narration-To-Image Rule

Do not illustrate every noun literally at the exact instant it is spoken. The image should usually lead or support the idea rather than behave like karaoke.

Preferred relationships:

- **anticipation:** visual idea appears just before narration names it;
- **confirmation:** visual settles as narration clarifies meaning;
- **afterimage:** image remains briefly after the phrase so the audience can absorb it.

Use literal synchronization sparingly for high-value moments such as Dilmun reveal, Nammu recognition, and the final civilization line.

## Shot 1 — 0-6s — Black Water Before Dawn

Narrative purpose: before civilization, before certainty.

Attention plan:

- first 1-2 seconds: almost no information beyond dark water and horizon;
- middle: faint gold line or mist separation becomes readable;
- end: enough vessel/world evidence appears to motivate the voyage shot.

Motion:

- camera drift/push remains barely perceptible;
- water and mist should be more visible than camera movement;
- no decorative particles.

Sound:

- water should establish place before score becomes assertive;
- allow low-frequency ambience and space;
- avoid beginning with a dramatic trailer hit.

Viewer question created: `Where are we?`

## Shot 2 — 6-13s — Stag Of The Absu

Narrative purpose: answer the first question with a practical journey.

Attention plan:

- vessel silhouette first;
- cargo/material detail second;
- direction of travel third.

Motion:

- strongest physical lateral movement in the first half of the reel;
- boat, water, and rigging must not share one transform;
- foreground occlusion can prepare Shot 3.

Sound:

- hull creak, rigging tension, water against vessel;
- one subtle score pulse can support scale.

Viewer question created: `Who is traveling, and why?`

## Shot 3 — 13-20s — Enki At The Helm

Narrative purpose: give the voyage a human/divine center.

Attention plan:

- Enki face becomes dominant immediately;
- boat context remains visible but subordinate;
- character motion should not announce itself.

Motion:

- slow push;
- boat settle;
- breath;
- one blink;
- optional tiny gaze shift;
- reflected water light becomes slightly more important near transition out.

Stillness moment:

The face should remain visually stable enough to create trust.

Sound:

- narration remains dominant;
- no sonic cue for blink or gaze;
- water may increase subtly before Nammu.

Viewer question created: `What is calling him?`

## Shot 4 — 20-28s — Nammu Beneath The Water

Narrative purpose: break the physical grammar established by the first three shots.

Attention plan:

- ordinary water remains believable;
- secondary current/light patterns slowly become coherent;
- audience recognizes presence without a literal apparition event.

Motion:

- camera nearly stops;
- physical water continues;
- divine current moves at a different rhythm;
- no fast opacity reveal.

Sound:

- open-water ambience narrows or deepens;
- score may thin rather than grow;
- use space and low detail to create awe.

Viewer realization: `The world contains more than Enki expected.`

## Shot 5 — 28-36s — Traveler Shrine / Hospitality

Narrative purpose: translate divine instruction into human dignity.

Attention plan:

- first: water source or basin;
- second: bread/welcome action;
- third: lived environment.

Motion:

- restrained camera push through a foreground element;
- one practical human action is better than many background loops;
- smoke/cloth/environment support depth.

Sound:

- water remains a thematic bridge from Nammu;
- small practical sounds may enter: fire, bread, footsteps, vessels.

Viewer understanding: `The instruction is about how people should live.`

## Shot 6 — 36-44s — Water, Bread, Truth, Justice, Freedom

Narrative purpose: turn abstract ideals into tangible civilization.

Attention plan:

Do not show every symbol simultaneously. Use serial focus.

Suggested order:

1. water/basin;
2. bread;
3. tablet or record;
4. scales or fair measure;
5. road/open passage.

Motion:

- object-to-object visual handoff;
- light or compositional movement can guide the eye;
- avoid infographic icon animation.

Sound:

- score can become slightly more structured;
- do not punctuate every noun with a hit.

Viewer understanding: `Civilization is practical behavior, not only monuments.`

## Shot 7 — 44-53s — Dilmun Reveal

Narrative purpose: visual release and discovery.

Attention plan:

- foreground opens;
- coastline appears;
- habitation and abundance become readable after the landscape itself.

Motion:

- strongest environmental reveal of the reel;
- haze, water, sparse birds, reeds provide scale;
- camera move should end before the shot does so the viewer can look.

Color:

- warmth and brightness peak here, not earlier.

Sound:

- score can widen;
- ambience transitions toward shore/land life;
- this is one appropriate place for a restrained musical rise.

Viewer response: `This journey has a destination.`

## Shot 8 — 53-60s — Arrival / Title

Narrative purpose: resolve motion while implying the story is only beginning.

Attention plan:

- boat/shore first;
- then title zone becomes dominant;
- final frame must have breathing room.

Motion:

- approach slows;
- environmental motion continues beneath the title;
- camera settles before the final beat;
- avoid a hard freeze unless intentionally designed.

Sound:

- final rise resolves without becoming triumphant trailer music;
- allow enough tail for the last narration thought to land.

Viewer response: `This is a beginning, not an ending.`

## Attention Budget Rules

- Never ask the viewer to read a caption, inspect a face, follow a foreground wipe, and notice a lighting event at the same instant.
- Major character action and major camera acceleration should not peak together unless narratively necessary.
- A transition should inherit one visual idea from the previous shot and establish one idea from the next.
- Each shot should contain at least one quiet interval where the composition can be read.
- The last 10-20% of most shots should prepare the next shot rather than introducing a new unrelated effect.

## Timing Validation Workflow

Once the preferred narration voice is approved:

1. use real word/phrase timings from the narration asset;
2. mark narration thought boundaries;
3. align camera accelerations and settles to those boundaries;
4. place character micro-performance between, not directly on top of, major narration beats;
5. test captions with the actual final text;
6. review the reel muted to verify visual logic;
7. restore audio and verify that sound increases meaning rather than merely energy.

## Definition Of Success

The reel should feel paced by thought, not by a timer. Each shot should hand attention to the next with a clear reason, and the audience should remember the voyage, Enki, Nammu, hospitality, and Dilmun rather than the animation techniques used to present them.
