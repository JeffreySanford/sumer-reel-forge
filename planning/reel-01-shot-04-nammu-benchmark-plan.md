# Reel 1 Shot 4 - Nammu Beneath The Water Benchmark Plan

## Purpose

Define the second Reel 1 animation benchmark: a scene that proves the approved motion system can handle the supernatural without relying on spectacle, literal character animation, or generic fantasy effects.

Shot 3 tests physical credibility. Shot 4 tests whether the project can deliberately depart from that physical grammar while remaining visually coherent.

## Core Series Principle

Make the ordinary world believable enough that the mythology feels extraordinary.

Shots 1-3 should establish weight, water, wood, cloth, human presence, and camera inertia. Shot 4 becomes powerful because those rules are already trusted.

Nammu should therefore feel different not because the scene suddenly becomes louder, brighter, or more effects-heavy, but because the movement no longer follows the same physical logic.

## Story Function

The audience should experience:

1. physical water;
2. a subtle visual pattern that seems unusual;
3. growing coherence;
4. recognition of feminine/divine presence;
5. uncertainty about whether she appeared or was always there.

The desired emotional read is awe and recognition, not fear.

## Target Duration

Current Reel 1 structure gives Shot 4 approximately 8 seconds.

Benchmark candidates may include 1-2 seconds of transition context from Shot 3 and into Shot 5, producing a 10-12 second review clip.

## Composition

### Primary eye target

Not a literal face at frame start.

The initial eye target should be a region of water/current/light that gradually becomes meaningful.

### Secondary eye target

Depth cues in the water: particles, surface/refraction, faint contour, negative space.

### Stillness anchor

The camera/composition itself.

Shot 3 uses slow physical camera motion. Shot 4 should noticeably quiet the camera so the audience senses a change before consciously identifying Nammu.

## Visual Strategy

Prefer environmental emergence over character entrance.

Strong candidates:

- light contours slowly align into facial suggestion;
- current lines become bilateral or organized enough to imply form;
- negative space suggests eyes/cheek/shoulder without drawing them explicitly;
- suspended light reveals and conceals portions of a presence;
- one region of water moves independently from the physical wave rhythm.

Avoid:

- transparent woman fading in;
- mermaid silhouette;
- glowing eyes;
- horror apparition;
- fantasy aura around a complete figure;
- sudden particle explosion;
- obvious morphing face effect.

## Layer Plan

Recommended minimum:

1. `deep-water-background`
2. `mid-current-plane`
3. `surface-refraction-or-caustic-plane`
4. `foreground-water-or-distortion`
5. `suspended-particles`
6. `nammu-coherence-mask`
7. `nammu-light-contour` if the mask alone is too abstract

Optional:

- faint partial eye/face reference layer at very low opacity;
- distant surface light;
- localized foam/current shape connecting back to Reel 1 visual bible.

Do not build Nammu as a conventional full-body character rig for this shot.

## Depth Plan

Conceptual values:

- deep background: `0.05`
- slow current: `0.18`
- Nammu coherence: `0.35-0.50`
- refraction/light: `0.55`
- foreground water/distortion: `0.80`
- near suspended particles: `0.90`

The presence should feel embedded in depth, not placed on top of the frame.

## Camera Plan

Primary camera preset: `drift` or near-static.

Candidate:

- scale: `1.000 -> 1.010` maximum;
- x/y travel: tiny and slow;
- no rotation;
- no obvious zoom toward the face.

If the camera movement is noticeable on first viewing, reduce it.

## Physical Water Motion

Physical water continues to obey established Reel 1 rules:

- layered multi-frequency movement;
- reflections/refraction respond to surface structure;
- particles drift with plausible local currents;
- no synchronized whole-screen wave.

This physical layer creates the baseline against which Nammu's movement is perceived as different.

## Numinous Motion

Use `numinousDrift` or a related preset with characteristics such as:

- slower than surrounding physical water;
- slightly independent direction;
- non-repeating timing;
- shape coherence that increases gradually;
- local light movement not obviously tied to a visible source;
- form that can dissolve back into water without a hard boundary.

The supernatural should feel integrated with the environment but not fully governed by it.

## Emergence Timeline

### Opening 0-25%

- viewer sees credible deep water;
- physical motion is established;
- Nammu is not yet consciously identifiable;
- sound shifts subtly from open-water realism toward depth/interiority.

### Recognition 25-60%

- current/light begins to align;
- one or two facial/form cues become readable;
- do not reveal all features equally;
- keep edges incomplete.

### Meaning 60-85%

- strongest coherence occurs here;
- the audience should be able to recognize feminine presence without needing a literal portrait;
- camera remains restrained.

### Dissolution / handoff 85-100%

- coherence starts returning to water;
- one bright water/reflection shape prepares the basin/spring transition into Shot 5;
- do not end on a frozen supernatural face.

## Light Language

Color:

- deep blue/cyan base;
- restrained pale luminous accents;
- no warm gold halo around Nammu.

Light should feel suspended in water rather than projected from a spotlight.

Potential techniques:

- low-frequency caustic masks;
- localized luminous contour;
- subtle depth-dependent blur;
- refraction displacement;
- slow brightness coherence in negative-space regions.

## Face / Identity Rule

Nammu's identity is an environmental presence first.

If a visible face is used:

- keep it partial;
- preserve the visual-bible description;
- avoid conventional beauty-shot framing;
- avoid a sharp cutout edge;
- avoid exact lip/eye animation;
- allow water/light to conceal portions of the face.

The audience should feel they are recognizing Nammu in the sea, not watching Nammu stand underwater.

## Transition In From Shot 3

Preferred handoff:

1. Enki settles;
2. reflected water light becomes more important;
3. camera movement quiets;
4. water/reflection fills enough of frame to motivate the cut;
5. Shot 4 begins already inside the visual language of that reflection.

The transition should shift attention downward/inward without a generic dissolve.

## Transition Out To Shot 5

Preferred handoff:

1. Nammu coherence dissolves;
2. a clear bright water shape remains;
3. shape/brightness matches to a spring, basin, or poured water in Shot 5;
4. physical human sound returns gradually.

This creates a thematic bridge: primordial water becomes water offered to people.

## Sound Plan

Sound can carry a large portion of the supernatural shift.

Candidate approach:

- reduce high-frequency open-air ambience;
- emphasize low water resonance;
- narrow or soften the score;
- introduce a nearly subliminal tonal layer;
- avoid horror stingers;
- avoid obvious magical chimes;
- allow a brief moment of relative quiet around recognition.

The supernatural should be felt as a change in acoustic space, not announced with an effect.

## Caption Integration

Keep captions especially stable in this shot.

The image already contains ambiguous motion; animated captions would compete with recognition.

Avoid placing text over the strongest Nammu coherence region.

## A/B Matrix

### A — Literalness

- A1: current/light only
- A2: current/light + very faint partial face reference

Prefer A1 unless A2 clearly improves recognition without reducing mystery.

### B — Camera

- B1: static
- B2: 1% slow drift

### C — Coherence peak

- C1: subtle, brief
- C2: slightly clearer, longer

### D — Particles

- D1: sparse
- D2: moderate

### E — Sound space

- E1: physical water dominant
- E2: physical water + low numinous tonal layer

Change one major variable at a time.

## Hard Fail Conditions

Reject the candidate if:

- Nammu looks like a mermaid;
- the shot reads as horror;
- the supernatural effect is primarily a glow filter;
- a literal face fades in through opacity;
- camera zoom is used to manufacture importance;
- particle density becomes decorative spectacle;
- the water ceases to feel physical;
- the effect resembles a generic fantasy template;
- the transition into Shot 5 loses the water thematic link.

## Review Questions

- Does the scene feel different from Shot 3 before Nammu is consciously recognized?
- Is physical water still believable?
- Does Nammu emerge from the environment rather than enter it?
- Is recognition possible on a phone screen?
- Is mystery preserved after recognition?
- Does the shot remain beautiful when paused?
- Is the effect stronger because it is restrained?
- Does the transition make primordial water and human hospitality feel connected?

## Pass Criteria

Shot 4 passes when:

- no hard-fail conditions occur;
- physical water and numinous motion are perceptibly different;
- the camera is calmer than Shot 3;
- Nammu is recognizable without conventional character staging;
- the scene scores at least 4/5 for atmosphere, originality, emotional read, transition quality, and publication readiness;
- beginning/middle/end frames are visually intentional;
- the scene feels authored specifically for Nammu and cannot be mistaken for a generic magical-water effect;
- we would willingly use it as the second visual benchmark for the series.

## What This Shot Proves

Shot 3 proves Sumer Reel Forge can make the physical world feel alive.

Shot 4 proves it can use that credibility to make mythology feel extraordinary without abandoning restraint, continuity, or the illustrated documentary language.

If both benchmarks work, the project has demonstrated the two visual grammars needed for much of `Blessings of Sumer`: believable physical life and restrained numinous intervention.
