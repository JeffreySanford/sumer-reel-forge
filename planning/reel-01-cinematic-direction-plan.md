# Reel 1 Cinematic Direction Plan

## Purpose

Define the visual, editorial, and motion decisions that should be made before more Reel 1 animation is implemented. Sprint 005 proved the pipeline. Sprint 006 should now optimize for perceived quality: composition, depth, camera intent, material realism, lighting, atmosphere, restrained character performance, transitions, and sound-image coordination.

This document complements `reel-01-animation-style-bible.md`. The style bible defines the governing rules; this plan turns those rules into a concrete directorial approach for `The Voyage Begins`.

## North-Star Test

A viewer should not think "this image has been moved around." The viewer should perceive a composed illustrated scene with depth, atmosphere, and intentional cinematic movement.

A representative frame should work as a still image. Motion should then add meaning, depth, or life without weakening the frame.

## Reel-Level Emotional Arc

Reel 1 should progress visually from uncertainty to purpose.

1. **Mystery** — black water, minimal information, restrained light.
2. **Journey** — motion becomes legible; the Stag establishes practical expedition scale.
3. **Character** — Enki becomes the visual anchor.
4. **Awe** — Nammu interrupts ordinary physical motion with an uncanny but restrained visual language.
5. **Purpose** — hospitality and civic duties become imaginable.
6. **Meaning** — symbolic objects become a coherent moral/civilizational vocabulary.
7. **Discovery** — Dilmun opens the visual field and warms the palette.
8. **Resolution** — movement converges toward land; the final composition allows the title to settle rather than compete.

The reel should therefore become gradually warmer, brighter, more spatially open, and more purposeful.

## Color Script

The color progression should be planned across the reel rather than graded independently shot by shot.

| Shot | Dominant temperature | Accent | Emotional function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | near-black blue / cool teal | faint reed gold | uncertainty |
| 2 | cool blue-green | copper / warm sail light | journey |
| 3 | balanced cool water + warm skin | lapis / copper | human focus |
| 4 | deep blue / cyan | pale suspended light | primordial awe |
| 5 | clay / limestone / fire warmth | water blue | hospitality |
| 6 | neutral earth + gold | lapis | ideals become concrete |
| 7 | warm limestone / reed gold | clear water blue | discovery |
| 8 | warm gold + open blue | copper / cream | arrival and resolution |

### Color rule

Do not let every shot reach maximum saturation or contrast. Reserve the strongest warmth and openness for Dilmun and the arrival.

## Camera Grammar

Camera movement should be limited to a small reusable vocabulary.

### Approved moves

- `slowPush`: 2-4% scale increase over 6-10 seconds.
- `lateralTrack`: restrained horizontal movement with parallax, used for travel.
- `riseReveal`: gentle upward camera movement that reveals an environment.
- `settle`: slight movement that decelerates into near-stillness for a line or title.
- `drift`: extremely slow positional movement used for mystery or suspended time.

### Avoid by default

- rapid zooms;
- constant Ken Burns movement on every layer;
- handheld-style shake;
- unmotivated camera reversals;
- large rotations;
- identical push-ins on every shot.

### Lens feeling

The compositions should imply a modest cinematic lens language even though the scenes are illustrated:

- wider environmental feeling for shots 1, 2, 7, and 8;
- more intimate, mildly compressed framing for Shot 3;
- dreamlike spatial ambiguity for Shot 4;
- stable documentary framing for Shots 5 and 6.

Do not simulate extreme depth of field merely because it is possible. Use blur mainly to separate foreground occluders or atmosphere, not to imitate a phone portrait mode.

## Motion Curve Library

Motion should share recognizable timing curves.

### `cinematicSlow`

For camera pushes and environment drift. Long ease-in, almost linear middle, soft ease-out.

### `heavyPhysical`

For boat and body settling. Movement overshoots slightly and returns slowly. The effect must be subtle.

### `clothLag`

Cloth follows body or wind motion with a short delay and lower amplitude.

### `waterPulse`

Non-repeating layered sine movement. Avoid one obvious repeating wave frequency.

### `numinousDrift`

For Nammu and divine light. Movement should not appear mechanically attached to gravity or the boat's physical rhythm.

These should become named motion presets rather than arbitrary interpolation values scattered through shot components.

## Material Motion Rules

Different materials should move differently.

- **Water:** layered, continuous, multi-frequency, reflective.
- **Reeds:** flexible, wind-driven, slightly delayed from one another.
- **Wool/linen:** low-amplitude cloth lag, not flag-like flapping.
- **Rigging:** small tension movement; no rubbery deformation.
- **Mist:** slow lateral or rising drift, low opacity, irregular density.
- **Dust:** sparse and nearly imperceptible unless strongly backlit.
- **Wood/reed vessel:** rigid body motion with separate rigging and cargo response.
- **Divine light:** slow, independent, non-physical movement.

Material-specific movement is one of the easiest ways to make layered animation feel more expensive.

## Character Micro-Performance: Enki

Enki should never look like a puppet waiting for his next animation cue.

For Shot 3, use a restrained performance sequence:

1. Start already alive: body at a natural breathing phase, eyes open, gaze toward travel direction.
2. Small breathing cycle, below conscious viewer attention.
3. One blink at an asymmetrical, non-round-number moment.
4. One small gaze or head adjustment after a narration idea changes.
5. Robe or hair responds slightly later than body movement.
6. End in a more settled pose than the beginning.

Do not animate the mouth during narrator-only passages.

### Face rule

The face is a continuity anchor. If layer separation, warping, or motion causes identity drift, reduce the motion rather than accepting the distortion.

## Nammu Motion Language

Shot 4 should intentionally break the physical rules established by Shots 1-3.

- The camera nearly stops.
- Ordinary water continues moving.
- A secondary pattern of light/current moves independently.
- Nammu appears through coherence rather than a literal pop-in: currents, highlights, and negative space gradually suggest presence.
- Her motion should be slower than expected and should not synchronize perfectly with waves.
- Avoid horror stingers, rapid opacity pulses, glowing eyes, or conventional mermaid staging.

The desired response is recognition followed by uncertainty: "Was she already there?"

## Environment Scale Rules

### Boat scenes

Use visible foreground boat structure, rigging, or cargo to provide scale. The Stag should remain a working vessel with weight and material texture.

### Dilmun

Dilmun should feel larger because the frame opens, not because the architecture becomes monumental fantasy. Depth comes from haze, shore layers, birds, vegetation, reflections, and atmospheric perspective.

### Shrine / hospitality future

Use lived-in practical details: water basin, oven, bread, woven shelter, lamps, people or evidence of people. Avoid sterile symbolic tableaux.

## Transition Plan

Transitions should be motivated by scene content where practical.

| From | To | Preferred transition idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 → 2 | darkness to voyage | mist clears or horizon light exposes the Stag |
| 2 → 3 | vessel to Enki | rigging/boat structure crosses frame and becomes foreground of closer composition |
| 3 → 4 | Enki to Nammu | reflected light/water fills frame; physical camera motion quiets |
| 4 → 5 | primordial water to human purpose | bright water reflection resolves into basin/spring water |
| 5 → 6 | hospitality to civic ideals | practical object match cut: bread/basin/tablet |
| 6 → 7 | ideals to place | light or road direction opens into Dilmun horizon |
| 7 → 8 | reveal to arrival | continuous directional movement toward coast |

Generic crossfades remain acceptable as fallback but should not be the default visual language.

## Foreground Occlusion Strategy

At least several shots should include a foreground object that partially crosses the camera or frames the subject. This can add depth without extra character animation.

Candidates:

- rigging or sail edge in Shot 2/3;
- reed shadow or vessel edge in Shot 3;
- water/refraction layer in Shot 4;
- doorway, hanging textile, or smoke in Shot 5;
- foreground reeds in Shot 7;
- boat structure or reed cluster in Shot 8.

Occlusion should be irregular and compositionally useful, not a repeated decorative wipe.

## Atmosphere Palette

Create a small reusable atmosphere library instead of inventing effects per shot.

- dawn mist;
- water glints;
- soft suspended dust;
- warm backlight haze;
- cool underwater particles;
- subtle reed shadow;
- soft reflected water light;
- restrained light rays.

Each shot should use no more than one or two dominant atmosphere treatments.

## Sound-to-Motion Planning

Visual motion should occasionally acknowledge audio without becoming music visualization.

Potential cues:

- low boat creak aligns with a small settle or rigging movement;
- water ambience grows slightly during Nammu transition;
- low drum can support major visual reveals rather than every cut;
- final musical rise should support the Dilmun/arrival expansion;
- silence or reduced ambience before a key narration phrase can make a nearly still image feel more dramatic.

Audio should be treated as part of perceived animation quality.

## Shot Intent Cards

Before implementation, each shot should have a short intent card containing:

- emotional purpose;
- primary subject;
- viewer eye target;
- primary motion;
- secondary motions;
- camera preset;
- depth planes;
- lighting intent;
- atmosphere choice;
- transition in;
- transition out;
- hard continuity constraints;
- one thing that must remain still.

The "one thing that must remain still" requirement is intentional. It protects the composition from unnecessary animation.

## Proposed Reel 1 Intent Summary

### Shot 1 — Black Water

- Eye target: faint horizon.
- Primary motion: very slow forward drift.
- Secondary: low mist, sparse water glints.
- Still anchor: horizon line.
- Goal: create curiosity with minimal information.

### Shot 2 — Stag Voyage

- Eye target: vessel silhouette/cargo.
- Primary motion: lateral travel.
- Secondary: boat bob, rigging response, water motion.
- Still anchor: distant horizon.
- Goal: make the expedition practical and physical.

### Shot 3 — Enki At The Helm

- Eye target: Enki's face.
- Primary motion: slow push.
- Secondary: boat settle, breathing, blink/gaze, water reflection.
- Still anchor: facial identity.
- Goal: establish calm purposeful authority.

### Shot 4 — Nammu

- Eye target: suggested face/presence in current/light.
- Primary motion: numinous emergence.
- Secondary: independent current and suspended light.
- Still anchor: overall composition/camera.
- Goal: awe without spectacle.

### Shot 5 — Traveler Shrine

- Eye target: water/bread/welcome action.
- Primary motion: restrained push through foreground.
- Secondary: smoke, cloth, small human/environment movement.
- Still anchor: shrine structure.
- Goal: turn divine instruction into lived human dignity.

### Shot 6 — Ideals

- Eye target: practical symbolic sequence.
- Primary motion: staged layered reveal or match movement.
- Secondary: light movement, subtle object parallax.
- Still anchor: one central object at a time.
- Goal: avoid looking like an infographic while communicating concepts.

### Shot 7 — Dilmun Reveal

- Eye target: coastline/habitation.
- Primary motion: rise reveal or frame opening.
- Secondary: haze, birds, water reflection, reeds.
- Still anchor: distant architecture/coast.
- Goal: visual release after tighter earlier compositions.

### Shot 8 — Arrival

- Eye target: Stag approaching land, then title.
- Primary motion: approach that settles.
- Secondary: water, rigging, light.
- Still anchor: final title zone.
- Goal: resolve motion and leave visual breathing room.

## Quality Failure Modes

Reject or revise a shot if it exhibits any of the following:

- looks like a flattened still with a zoom applied;
- every layer moves at the same speed;
- obvious looping sine-wave motion;
- character identity shifts between states;
- too many effects compete for attention;
- foreground motion obscures the subject without purpose;
- excessive glow or fantasy spectacle;
- transitions feel like templates rather than story-driven edits;
- camera movement continues merely because the shot has time remaining;
- visual focus competes with captions;
- motion makes material behave incorrectly;
- a technically clever effect draws attention to itself instead of the story.

## Iteration Strategy

Use controlled A/B comparisons rather than broad rewrites.

For Shot 3, compare one variable at a time:

- camera push 2% vs 4%;
- 4 depth planes vs 6;
- foreground rigging present vs absent;
- blink only vs blink + gaze shift;
- reflection mask low vs medium intensity;
- mist absent vs subtle;
- more literal vs more restrained cloth motion.

Keep the stronger version and record the decision. Avoid changing camera, lighting, character motion, and atmosphere simultaneously between review candidates.

## Publication-Quality Gate

A benchmark scene should pass all of these before style propagation:

- strong still frame at beginning, middle, and end;
- clear subject within 0.5 seconds;
- depth readable without exaggerated parallax;
- camera movement feels intentional and nearly invisible;
- character remains visually consistent;
- at least two independent environmental motion channels;
- no obvious procedural/vector look unless intentionally part of the art style;
- captions remain subordinate to the image;
- transition in/out is motivated;
- scene works with audio muted;
- scene becomes noticeably better when audio is restored;
- no reviewer describes the result as a slideshow, puppet, infographic, or template.

## Decision

Do not solve quality by adding more animation features. Improve quality first through stronger source artwork, layer preparation, composition, motion restraint, coherent timing, material behavior, and directorial review. Reel 1 remains the laboratory until this language is proven.
