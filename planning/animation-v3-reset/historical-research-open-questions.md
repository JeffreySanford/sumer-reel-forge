# Historical Research Open Questions and Stop Conditions

Status: **final research backlog before production-specific investigation**  
Research date: **2026-08-25**

This document intentionally lists what the final Internet/source audit did **not** settle. Planning is considered complete even though historical research remains open: these questions should be answered only when a specific scene, asset or manuscript revision makes them material.

## 1. Exact project chronology

The single highest-value unresolved research question is the intended historical-period band for each major manuscript unit.

The story is mythic historical fiction and deliberately compresses traditions, but production still needs approximate bands such as:

```text
MYTHIC / NON-HISTORICAL FRAME
Late Uruk / Jemdet Nasr context
Early Dynastic
Akkadian
Ur III
Old Babylonian literary reception
```

Without this, questions like horses, Gulf trade, city scale, metallurgy, shoreline and temple form cannot have one correct answer.

### Stop condition

Do not attempt one global manuscript date. Assign period relationships per production unit/source/visual claim.

## 2. Paleogeography / shoreline / river courses

Open questions:

- Gulf shoreline relative to Eridu and Ur at the selected scene dates;
- Euphrates/Tigris distributary channels;
- which canals are archaeologically visible vs textually named;
- navigation routes among target cities;
- marsh extent and seasonal variability.

### Required source type

Peer-reviewed geoarchaeology, remote sensing, excavation reports and dated paleochannel studies.

### Stop condition

Do not freeze precise Level 3 geography from modern maps.

## 3. Exact Eridu/E-Absu architectural reconstruction

Literary descriptions are rich but metaphorical. Archaeology provides temple sequences, but the manuscript's luminous silver/lapis descriptions should not be naïvely rendered as literal entire-building cladding without material/evidence classification.

Open:

- which Eridu temple phase best informs project design;
- dimensions/entrances/platforms for chosen phase;
- relation to water/reedbeds;
- which literary motifs are symbolic vs materially plausible;
- evidence for color/plaster/ornament.

### Stop condition

Architecture kit may remain schematic until phase-specific research is selected.

## 4. Umma patron/cult mapping

Šara is commonly associated with Umma, but this final pass did not promote a dedicated current corpus page into the project's authority set.

### Action

Before canonical city-deity record, verify through ORACC/CDLI/royal inscription or current academic source.

## 5. Bad-tibira, Kuara, Kesh and secondary city cults

The manuscripts use many cities beyond the strongest primary set. Their patronage and economic specializations should be checked individually rather than extrapolated from later god lists.

## 6. Timber / cedar sourcing

The manuscript uses cedar/large timbers in southern construction.

Open:
- species terms in exact source text;
- import route and period;
- Zagros vs Lebanon vs other wood sources;
- ordinary architecture vs elite temple use.

### Stop condition

Do not depict southern alluvial cities as locally forested with cedar.

## 7. Food history

Lemon is clearly late and should be removed/reclassified. Other ingredients need period-specific checking before a “historically authentic meal” claim:

```text
chickpeas
sesame / sesame oil
garlic
onion/leek
cheese/dairy
bread grains
beer
cumin/coriander
fruit varieties
fish species
```

Old Babylonian recipe tablets are valuable analogues but later than much of the manuscript's implied world.

### Stop condition

Use `LATER_ANALOGUE` when later recipes inspire an earlier scene.

## 8. Equids by scene date

Kunga evidence is strong for third-millennium high-status equids, and domestic horses arrive late enough to matter.

Open:
- exact date of each manuscript procession/travel scene;
- whether “horse” is generic literary shorthand or intended zoological horse;
- visual differentiation among donkey, onager/hemione and kunga.

### Stop condition

No historically labeled pre-late-third-millennium horse asset without specific evidence.

## 9. Animal species in literary gift lists

Translations of ancient animal terms are sometimes uncertain.

Open:
- gazelle/ibex/deer distinctions;
- cattle varieties;
- sheep/goat morphology;
- exotic animals in trade/gift lists;
- whether a translation has `(?)` uncertainty.

### Stop condition

Fixture preserves source uncertainty rather than choosing species solely for visual appeal.

## 10. Sacred marriage enactment

Texts and ritual context are real; exact human enactment remains debated.

### Stop condition

No production label `HISTORICAL_FACT_LITERAL_INTERCOURSE`. Use `DEBATED_RITUAL_INTERPRETATION` unless a scene is explicitly fictionalizing a particular scholarly reconstruction.

## 11. Divine genealogy variants

Open by design, not solvable globally.

Examples:
- parentage of Enki;
- parentage of Inanna;
- children of Enlil by tradition;
- Nergal/Ereshkigal relationship chronology;
- Namma's cosmogonic role.

### Stop condition

Model source-specific edges plus project-continuity genealogy. Never seek one “true Sumerian family tree.”

## 12. Uttu vs Inanna naming/confusion

The manuscripts use `Uttu` in places where narrative function appears closer to Inanna/Uruk, while Uttu herself has genuine textile significance.

### Action

Perform manuscript-level entity pass before final Chapter 1/3 publication:

```text
Utu   = sun god
Uttu  = goddess associated with weaving / Enki-Ninhursag cycle
Inanna = Uruk goddess / Venus / love-war complex
```

This is partly historical and partly copy/entity disambiguation.

## 13. Calendars and festivals

Historical calendars were local and changed over time.

Open:
- which city's calendar applies to each scene;
- festival month names;
- intercalation in target period;
- whether project 13-moon motifs are explicitly symbolic.

### Stop condition

Do not build one universal “Sumerian calendar” component until a target period/city is selected.

## 14. Ancient magic by period

Mesopotamian magic/incantation is historically real, but much richly preserved anti-witchcraft material is first millennium.

Open:
- which incantation types are attested in Early Dynastic/Ur III/Old Babylonian periods;
- ritual specialists and terminology by period;
- images/figurines/materials appropriate to target period.

### Stop condition

Later Maqlû material is `LATER_ANALOGUE` unless scene is later.

## 15. Tree-of-Life symbolic redesign

The audit established that several modern correspondences are striking, but the city order should no longer force false historical patronage.

Open design problem:

```text
Should Chapter 3's Tree use:
  A) cities as the ten nodes, historical deity shown separately;
  B) deity/planet principles as nodes, cities as narrative stations;
  C) dual-layer map: historical city network + symbolic Tree overlay?
```

Recommendation: **C**. It preserves both systems without making either one lie.

## 16. Archaeological image rights

Historical evidence quality and permission to ingest/display an image are separate.

Before a museum image becomes production art reference rather than metadata-only:

- confirm image license;
- record credit requirements;
- classify commercial/noncommercial restrictions;
- retain object-page URL even if image bytes cannot be stored.

## 17. Research stop rule

The platform should not research forever.

A production unit is historically `RESEARCH_READY` when:

```text
narrative source relationship classified
major deity/city claims checked
period band stated or intentionally mythic
material-culture red flags checked
visual evidence has at least one authoritative source or explicit uncertainty
modern symbolic overlays separated
open questions are non-blocking or recorded as blockers
```

Research reopens only when:

- a new scene makes an unresolved claim visible;
- a source correction materially changes interpretation;
- a benchmark needs real historical geometry/material;
- a reviewer challenges a claim with better evidence.

That is the boundary between rigorous research and endless preproduction.
