# Historical-Fiction Revision Recommendations — Chapters 1–3

Status: **optional manuscript/production corrections derived from final research audit**  
Research date: **2026-08-25**

These recommendations protect the author's fiction rather than flattening it into textbook prose. `CHANGE` means current wording/material would likely be interpreted as a historical claim contradicted by strong evidence. `RELABEL` means the fiction can remain if its provenance class becomes explicit.

## Status vocabulary

```text
KEEP
  source/cultural foundation is strong

KEEP_AND_STRENGTHEN
  strong evidence exists and can be exposed more visibly

RELABEL_ADAPTATION
  retain story decision but mark source divergence

RELABEL_SYMBOLIC
  retain modern/esoteric correspondence, keep separate from historical evidence

CHANGE_RECOMMENDED
  targeted correction improves historical credibility without harming narrative

DEBATED
  preserve ambiguity / avoid categorical claim

RESEARCH_MORE
  exact answer depends on scene date/site/specific evidence
```

## Small precision improvements with large credibility payoff

### 1. Use tradition-qualified genealogy

Instead of:

> Utu was the son of X and therefore...

Prefer internally:

```text
source tradition: hymn A / Enlil-and-Ninlil / god-list B
project continuity: chosen genealogy
```

This lets the prose remain natural while V3 keeps the scholarly nuance.

### 2. Stop using modern English deity-domain labels as exclusive job titles

`god of war`, `goddess of writing`, etc. are useful shorthand, but Mesopotamian deities accumulate overlapping functions and change over time. Treat functions as many-to-many associations with evidence/periods.

### 3. Preserve uncertainty in species/material names

Ancient plant, animal, timber, textile and stone translations are often uncertain. If ETCSL uses `(?)`, production research should not silently upgrade that into certainty because it makes a prettier prop.

### 4. Date every technology-sensitive scene

Before visual research is approved, record an approximate period band. This matters for:

```text
horse vs kunga
bronze/copper technologies
Dilmun trade intensity
city size/layout
shoreline/river course
specific temple reconstruction
calendar administration
```

### 5. Preserve the author's deliberate source mashups

Several manuscript passages visibly splice ancient compositions together. That is not inherently a problem. The high-quality solution is:

```text
project scene
  → source A direct passage
  → source B motif
  → archaeological context C
  → deliberate bridge D
```

rather than trying to force the whole scene under one ancient text title.

## Recommended manuscript-edit policy

Do **not** automatically rewrite the manuscripts from this audit.

First convert audit findings into structured provenance. Then decide whether each discrepancy is:

```text
invisible back-end classification only
small terminology correction
visual-production correction
footnote/source note
intentional story divergence worth preserving
```

This protects the fact that the chapters were authored as historical fiction while substantially increasing the project's scholarly honesty.
