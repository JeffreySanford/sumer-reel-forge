# Reel 1 Animation Review Scorecard

## Purpose

Provide a repeatable review method for Reel 1 animation candidates so visual quality is judged deliberately rather than by vague impressions such as "better" or "more cinematic."

This scorecard is intended for Shot 3 benchmark reviews first, then Shot 4 or Shot 7, and finally the complete Reel 1 pass.

## Scoring

Score each category from 1 to 5.

- **1 — Reject:** clearly weak, distracting, broken, or stylistically wrong.
- **2 — Major revision:** idea may work but execution is visibly below target.
- **3 — Functional:** acceptable prototype quality, not publication quality.
- **4 — Strong:** publication-capable with minor polish.
- **5 — Benchmark:** visually convincing and reusable as the style reference.

A technically valid render can still fail this scorecard.

## Hard-Fail Conditions

Any hard fail blocks visual approval regardless of total score.

- Character identity visibly drifts.
- Critical subject is obscured by captions or foreground effects.
- Generated visual contains culturally inappropriate or clearly incorrect fantasy shorthand.
- Motion produces obvious clipping, broken transparency, layer seams, or warping.
- Scene looks materially implausible in a distracting way.
- Major camera movement is unmotivated or nauseating.
- Shot reads primarily as a slideshow, infographic, puppet, or generic template.
- The scene contradicts the project visual bible.

## Review Categories

### 1. Composition

Questions:

- Is the eye drawn quickly to the intended subject?
- Does the composition remain strong at the beginning, middle, and end?
- Is there negative space where captions or titles need it?
- Does motion preserve rather than destroy the original framing?

### 2. Depth And Parallax

Questions:

- Can foreground, subject, midground, and background be read clearly?
- Do near layers move more than distant layers?
- Is the parallax subtle enough to feel cinematic rather than game-like?
- Does depth remain coherent throughout camera movement?

### 3. Camera Language

Questions:

- Is there one clear dominant camera idea?
- Does the move start and stop deliberately?
- Is the amplitude restrained?
- Does the shot avoid generic perpetual zooming?

### 4. Character Continuity

Questions:

- Does Enki remain unmistakably the same person?
- Does head/eye/body layering remain aligned?
- Are pose changes and facial motion restrained?
- Is stillness preserved where it helps dignity and authority?

### 5. Character Life

Questions:

- Does the character feel alive without looking puppeted?
- Are blink, breath, gaze, cloth, and posture changes naturally timed?
- Is there asymmetry and variation rather than obvious looping?
- Does the animation support the narration rather than compete with it?

### 6. Environmental Motion

Questions:

- Do water, mist, reeds, rigging, dust, cloth, and light behave according to their material?
- Are at least two motion channels independent?
- Do loops avoid obvious repetition?
- Is environmental movement subordinate to the subject?

### 7. Lighting And Atmosphere

Questions:

- Is the lighting direction coherent?
- Does atmosphere create depth rather than merely adding effects?
- Is the warm/cool balance consistent with the reel color script?
- Are highlights and divine effects restrained?

### 8. Material Realism

Questions:

- Does the boat feel heavy and rigid?
- Does cloth lag and settle correctly?
- Does water move continuously rather than as a flat pattern?
- Do reeds, mist, wood, skin, and reflective surfaces behave differently?

### 9. Cultural / World Consistency

Questions:

- Does the scene remain grounded in the visual bible?
- Does it avoid Egyptian, Greco-Roman, medieval, or generic fantasy visual leakage?
- Do vessel, clothing, architecture, and objects remain plausible within the chosen Sumerian-inspired visual language?

### 10. Transition Quality

Questions:

- Does the transition arise naturally from scene content?
- Does it preserve direction, light, shape, or thematic continuity?
- Would a simple cut be stronger than the current effect?
- Does the transition avoid feeling like a preset?

### 11. Caption Integration

Questions:

- Are captions legible without dominating the composition?
- Are faces and critical objects unobstructed?
- Does caption timing support rather than interrupt the visual beat?
- Does the scene still look composed with captions visible?

### 12. Sound / Image Relationship

Questions:

- Does ambience make the visual feel more alive?
- Are major visual events supported by sound rather than over-scored?
- Is there space for important narration lines?
- Do audio changes help the emotional arc?

### 13. Originality / Template Avoidance

Questions:

- Does the shot feel authored for this story?
- Could the same animation be dropped into an unrelated reel without anyone noticing?
- Are motion choices specific to Enki, the Stag, Nammu, or Dilmun?
- Does the shot avoid generic social-video editing language?

### 14. Emotional Read

Questions:

- Is the intended emotion clear without reading production notes?
- Does Shot 3 read as calm authority?
- Does Shot 4 read as awe and recognition rather than horror?
- Does Shot 7 read as discovery and release?

### 15. Overall Publication Readiness

Questions:

- Would we post this clip by itself?
- Would the shot hold up on a phone screen?
- Would a paused frame still look intentional?
- Does the result feel more expensive than the technical complexity suggests?

## Score Thresholds

For the Shot 3 benchmark:

- No hard fails.
- No category below 3.
- Composition, camera, character continuity, depth, and overall publication readiness must each score at least 4.
- Average score should be at least 4.0 before the style is propagated.

For a final Reel 1 publication candidate:

- No hard fails in any shot.
- No shot average below 3.7.
- Reel-level average at least 4.0.
- Shot 3 and at least one contrasting benchmark shot at 4.2 or better.

These numbers are guides for disciplined review, not a substitute for judgment.

## A/B Review Template

For each benchmark iteration, record:

- Candidate A identifier / checksum.
- Candidate B identifier / checksum.
- Exactly one primary variable being compared.
- Score differences by category.
- Preferred candidate.
- Reason for decision.
- Whether the decision becomes a reusable style rule.

Example:

```text
Variable: camera push amplitude
A: 2%
B: 4%
Decision: A
Reason: 4% makes the face feel artificially enlarged and reveals layer separation.
Reusable rule: intimate character shots default to <= 3% push unless composition requires more.
```

## Phone Review

Every benchmark candidate should be watched:

1. full-screen on desktop with headphones;
2. phone-sized with speakers;
3. muted;
4. paused at 25%, 50%, and 75% of the shot.

A scene that only works large or only works with sound has not fully passed.

## Review Vocabulary

Prefer precise notes:

- "foreground rigging moves too fast relative to boat";
- "camera does not settle before narration beat";
- "water reflection mask competes with face";
- "blink is too frequent";
- "Nammu appears through opacity rather than water structure";
- "Dilmun warmth peaks too early".

Avoid vague notes when possible:

- "make cinematic";
- "needs more energy";
- "looks AI";
- "make it pop".

If a vague reaction occurs, translate it into composition, motion, material, continuity, lighting, or timing terms before implementation.

## Decision

Reel 1 visual approval is a human editorial decision supported by this scorecard. Automated technical validation can confirm codecs, dimensions, duration, checksums, and synchronization, but it cannot mark a shot visually approved.
